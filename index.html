Definition & Iron Physiology

Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA) is anemia caused by insufficient iron availability for erythropoiesis and hemoglobin synthesis.

Typical Laboratory Findings

  • Low Hemoglobin
  • Low MCV (Microcytic anemia)
  • Low Ferritin
  • Low Serum Iron
  • Low TSAT
  • High TIBC

Iron absorption mainly occurs in the duodenum and proximal jejunum.

Causes of Iron Deficiency

Blood Loss

  • GI bleeding
  • Peptic ulcer disease
  • Hemorrhoids
  • Menorrhagia
  • Blood donation

Increased Demand

  • Pregnancy
  • Rapid growth
  • Adolescence
  • Lactation

Reduced Absorption

  • Celiac disease
  • Bariatric surgery
  • H. pylori
  • PPI therapy

Diagnosis

Important Tests

  • CBC
  • Ferritin
  • Iron Studies
  • Reticulocyte Count
  • TSAT

Ferritin remains one of the most important diagnostic markers in IDA.

Treatment & Oral Iron Strategy

Modern Evidence-Based Oral Iron

  • Ferrous sulfate preferred
  • 40–80 mg elemental iron
  • Daily or alternate-day dosing
  • Better absorption with lower doses
  • Continue ~3 months after Hb normalization

CKD & Dialysis Guidelines

  • Functional iron deficiency common in CKD
  • IV iron preferred in many dialysis patients
  • ESA optimization requires adequate iron
  • Monitor Hb, ferritin, and TSAT
